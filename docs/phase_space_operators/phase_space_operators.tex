\title{Phase-space $R$, $Q$, $T$, $Q^{-1}$ for macroscopic interfaces\\[2pt]
\large Experimental formulation, for review before implementation}
\date{30 September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Every lattice operator of the library samples the bare field at the Nyquist
rate of its full angular band. For a macroscopic interface this is
$N\sim(2a\,\mathrm{NA}/\lambda)^2$ samples ($2\times10^8$ for the 4.6~mm
diopter), so no operator acting on it can run in seconds. A smooth macroscopic
field carries far less information: locally it is one plane wave with a slowly
varying envelope. We propose to represent fields on planes \emph{and on the
interface} as sums of local plane-wave atoms whose carriers are removed, and to
apply $R$, $Q$, $T$ and $Q^{-1}$ atom by atom, each to a prescribed tolerance.
The Nyquist lattice of the bare field is never formed. Cost and memory then
follow the field's phase-space content, not its size in wavelengths.
\end{abstract}

\section{What was learned}
\begin{itemize}
\item $T$ (local Fresnel) is accurate at macroscopic scale: against the exact
single-interface sphere its surface error falls from $5\times10^{-2}$
($ka=10^2$) to $8\times10^{-6}$ ($ka=3\times10^3$).
\item The explicit $Q^{-1}$ (Love equivalence, Weyl expansion, both traces, no
iterations) reproduces exact Maxwell fields to $6\times10^{-6}$ at $ka=10^3$;
the least-squares inverse gave $10^{-2}$.
\item Operators on the Nyquist lattice fail the runtime target however they
are organised: the global $Q$ took 277~s at $ka=3\times10^3$ ($8\times10^5$
nodes); a tiled $Q$ with tilted tangent planes was exact to $10^{-8}$ but took
22--37~s there and did not finish at $ka=10^4$. Its tiles still sampled the
carrier at $\lambda/2$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Representation}
On a plane $\Pi$ (coordinates $\ve x\in\mathbb R^2$) a field is
\begin{equation}
 \ve E(\ve x)=\sum_a \ve u_a(\ve x-\ve x_a)\,e^{i\kp_a\cdot\ve x},
 \label{eq:atoms}
\end{equation}
where each atom $a$ has a centre $\ve x_a$, a carrier $\kp_a$ and a smooth
vector envelope $\ve u_a$ supported (to tolerance) in a window of width $w$.
The envelope is sampled on its own small grid at the envelope Nyquist rate,
set by the window and by the residual local band, not by $k$. Atoms come from
a partition of unity $\sum_a\chi_a=1$ (smooth windows with Gaussian spectral
decay) applied to the field, each piece demodulated by its dominant local
wavevector; a piece with several significant local wavevectors (crossing
beams, near a caustic) splits into several atoms. Atoms below the tolerance
are dropped. Optionally the carrier also removes a local quadratic phase
(local wavefront curvature), which keeps envelopes smooth for converging or
diverging fields.

On the interface $\Sigma$ ($z=h(\ve x)$ over the chart) the trace has the same
form with chart carriers $\ve q_a$: the tangential phase gradient.

\paragraph{Why the size is small.} A smooth beam of aperture $D$ has at each
place a local band of width $\sim2\pi/w$ around one carrier. With windows of
Fresnel scale $w\sim\sqrt{\lambda L}$ ($L$ the distance over which the
envelope changes) the number of atoms is $\sim(D/w)^2$, each with a few hundred
envelope samples. For the diopter this is $10^3$--$10^5$ numbers instead of
$2\times10^8$.

\section{Operators on atoms}
\paragraph{$R_z$ (plane to plane, exact ASM).} An atom's angular spectrum is
its envelope spectrum shifted by $\kp_a$. Propagation multiplies it by
$e^{ik_z(\kp)z}$. Write
$k_z(\kp)=k_z(\kp_a)+\ve v_a\cdot(\kp-\kp_a)+\delta(\kp)$ with
$\ve v_a=-\kp_a/k_z(\kp_a)$: the constant is a phase, the linear term a shift
of the centre by $-\ve v_a z$ (the ray displacement), and $\delta$ the local
diffraction, applied exactly by one small FFT on the envelope grid. $R_z$ is
translation invariant, so all atoms with one carrier share one kernel.

\paragraph{$Q$ (plane to $\Sigma$).} For an atom with footprint centred at
$\ve x_f$ (its centre displaced along its ray to the surface), expand
$h(\ve x)=h_0+\ve g\cdot(\ve x-\ve x_f)+r(\ve x)$. The constant and tilt give
a phase and a new chart carrier $\ve q=\kp+k_z(\kp)\,\ve g$ (exactly, per
envelope frequency). The residual $r$ is small over one footprint, so
$e^{ik_z r}$ needs a low-rank Chebyshev expansion. The output is a chart
atom with carrier $\ve q_a$, sampled at envelope rate.

\paragraph{$T$ (local Fresnel).} Phase matching keeps the tangential phase
gradient continuous across $\Sigma$: $T$ does not change chart carriers. It
multiplies each envelope sample by the local $3\times3$ Fresnel matrix, which
varies slowly. Transmitted and reflected traces are chart atoms on each side.

\paragraph{$Q^{-1}$ (explicit, $\Sigma$ to plane).} For each chart atom,
Love's currents $\ve J=\ve n\times\ve H$, $\ve M=-\ve n\times\ve E$ radiate
into the region beyond $\Sigma$:
$\ve A(\kp)=-\frac{k}{8\pi^2k_z}\big[Z(\mathsf I-\hat k\hat k)\tilde{\ve J}
-\hat k\times\tilde{\ve M}\big]$, with $\tilde{\ve J}$ the surface integral
of $\ve J e^{-i\ve k\cdot\ve r}$ over the atom's footprint. Its band sits
around the refracted carrier; it is returned as one plane atom on the next
plane, centred at the back-projected footprint. No iterations.

\section{Error control}
Each truncation is bounded by one tolerance $\varepsilon$: window tails and
dropped atoms ($\le\varepsilon$ of the field norm); envelope band limits;
Chebyshev rank ($\le\varepsilon$ by the Bessel tail bound); per-atom FFT
aliasing (envelope grids hold the displaced, spread atom). Re-partitioning
after each operator keeps envelopes smooth. The only model error is the local
Fresnel $T$, measured separately against exact fields.

\section{Validation and targets}
\begin{itemize}
\item Each operator against the existing exact lattice operators ($R$, $Q$,
$Q^H$, explicit $Q^{-1}$) on small cells, to $\varepsilon$.
\item The full pipeline $R\,Q^{-1}TQ\,R$ against the exact single-interface
sphere for $ka=10^3\ldots10^5$ (both faces), and against the Müller and
PMCHWT solvers for non-spherical elements at mid scale.
\item Runtime: under five minutes is the acceptance limit for any stage;
the goal is under one second for a macroscopic interface, with compiled
per-atom kernels. Memory is reported against the field's own storage.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open questions}
\begin{itemize}
\item Window width: fixed Fresnel scale, or adapted per region from the
local wavefront curvature and the surface curvature.
\item Carrier model: linear phase only, or linear plus quadratic (local
wavefront curvature), which keeps envelopes small near foci.
\item Fields that are not locally single-valued (caustics, edges): more
atoms per window; the cost then grows with the true phase-space content.
\end{itemize}
